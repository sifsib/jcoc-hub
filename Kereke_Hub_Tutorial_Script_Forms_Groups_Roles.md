# Kereke Hub Tutorial Script
## Forms, Groups, and Roles Management

---

## PART: Forms

Forms is where you create any custom form your church needs. This could be membership forms, surveys, prayer request cards, event registrations, or anything else. The process is simple and flexible.

Go to Forms, create a new form, and give it a name. Then you add the fields you want to include. For each field, you decide whether it's required or optional.

Let's say you're creating a membership form. You start with first name, mark it required. Add last name, required. Add address as a long text field, required. Then email, phone number, date of birth. If you want to include checklists or multiple-choice questions, you can set those up too. Every field is customizable.

Once you've built the form and you're happy with it, you save it. The system generates a link. You send that link to your congregation, your members, or new joiners. They fill in the form from their device, and it all comes directly back to you.

Here's where it saves time: once the data comes in, you can view it right there, but you can also download it as CSV or Excel. Then you go to the membership section and upload the file directly. No manual typing. No re-entering information. Just a simple download and upload process. The data flows from your form into your member list automatically.

## PART: Groups

Groups is powerful if you have small groups, cell groups, departments, or ministry teams that need independent attendance tracking without getting lost in your overall church numbers.

The smart part is how you create them. You don't have to manually pick each person. Instead, you filter by almost any criteria you want. You can filter by date of birth, role, location, marital status, or any custom field you've added to your membership form.

Let's say you want to create a group of married men. You set your filters: marital status equals married, gender equals male. You apply those filters and create the group. The system automatically pulls everyone who matches that criteria. Now you have a group that stays updated automatically. If a new married man joins your church and fits those criteria, he'll be added to that group.

Then when you have a service or meeting for that specific group, you mark attendance. You're not manually selecting people, not building lists by hand. The group already contains exactly who needs to be there.

This approach saves enormous amounts of time, especially in larger churches where manual grouping becomes impractical. Filter once, create the group, and let the system keep it organized for you.

## PART: Roles and Access Control

Your church has different leaders doing different jobs, and they need different levels of access to the system. Roles let you give each person exactly what they need to see and do, nothing more, nothing less.

Here's what you can assign:

**Admin** has full access to everything in the system, just like you. They can see all the data, make changes, and manage settings. The only thing they can't do is delete people from the system, which protects against accidents.

**View Only** is perfect for someone who needs to see data but shouldn't be able to change anything. Maybe you have a board member who wants visibility into attendance or financials without being able to edit.

**Finance Manager** sees only the financial information. Tithes, offerings, donations, reports. They can't see membership data or attendance. They handle money, and that's all they see.

**Membership Manager** manages members and their records without accessing any financial data. They handle registrations, updates, and member information, but finances stay hidden.

**Cell Leader** only sees people in their specific region or group. If they're assigned twelve people in their area, they see those twelve people and nothing else. This lets them follow up with their group without seeing the whole congregation.

To assign a role, you enter the person's name and email, choose the role you want to give them, and hit send. They'll receive an email with a link to access the system with those permissions. You'll also get a copy of that link. I'd recommend copying it and sending it to them directly as well, just in case their email gets delayed or filters it out. It makes the onboarding smoother.

That's how you set up your team with the right access levels and keep your data secure at the same time.
